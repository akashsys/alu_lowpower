During post-synthesis verification, a **critical functional defect** has been identified: although an **asynchronous active-low reset signal (`rst_n`)** exists in the design, it is **disconnected from the internal Finite State Machine (FSM) registers** in the synthesized netlist. As a result, the ALU FSM powers up in an undefined state and may assert `busy` unpredictably.

## Design Architecture and Clocking Model

### Power Controller: `alu_clk_off`

The `alu_clk_off` module represents a simplified **power-management controller**, similar to what exists in a real SoC. In physical silicon, such a block would manage transitions between **Active** and **Sleep** modes by controlling clock and power enables.

### Clock Gating Behavior

* Power-off behavior is controlled by the signal `diss_clk`
* When `diss_clk` is asserted:

  * The internal clock enable (`alu_en`) must be forced LOW
  * No clock activity is allowed inside the ALU

### State Preservation Requirement

While `diss_clk` is high:

* All sequential logic must **retain its current state**
* FSM state registers and cycle-accurate counters must **not advance**
* No partial execution or unintended transitions are permitted

---

## Output Clamping and Isolation

### Why Clamping Is Required

In real silicon, when a power-gated domain is turned off:

* Internal node voltages collapse
* Outputs may become floating, resulting in **X or Z values**

If these undefined values propagate into always-on logic:

* Meta-stability may occur
* Illegal state transitions may be triggered
* Excessive current draw or functional failure can result

### Modeling Real Silicon Behavior

The `clamp_value` logic in the `top` module acts as an **Isolation Cell**:

* When `diss_clk` is asserted, outputs are forced to a known constant
* This ensures the rest of the system always observes a **clean, deterministic value** (typically 0)
* No floating or unknown values are allowed to escape the ALU power domain

## Functional Implementation Overview

### ALU Interface

* Operands: `A[15:0]`, `B[15:0]`
* Opcode: `opcode[3:0]`
* Control: `start`, `busy`

---

### Single-Cycle Operations

These operations complete immediately when `start` is sampled in the **IDLE** state:

* **Arithmetic / Logic**

  * Add (`0x0`)
  * Subtract (`0x1`)
  * AND / OR / XOR / NOR (`0x2–0x5`)
  * XNOR (`0x7`)

* **Shift**

  * Logical Left Shift (`0x6`)
  * Operand `A` shifted by `B[3:0]`

---

### Multi-Cycle Sequential Operations

These operations require accurate modeling of fixed hardware latency:

* **Multiply (`4'b1000`)**

  * Executes over exactly **4 clock cycles**
  * `busy` must remain asserted throughout
  * Result is latched only on the final cycle

* **Divide (`4'b1001`)**

  * Executes over exactly **8 clock cycles**
  * Includes safety logic:

    * Division-by-zero must produce a result of **zero**
  * `busy` remains asserted until completion

---


In `gate_netlist.v`, the reset signal `rst_n`:

* Exists at the top level
* Does **not** influence the FSM state registers
* Is effectively isolated from the sequential logic

---

### Your Task

You must **manually modify `gate_netlist.v`** to restore the **Reset Protocol with absolute priority**.

### Required Reset Behavior (Strict)

* Reset dominance is **non-negotiable**
* Whenever `rst_n` is asserted LOW:

  * The FSM must **immediately transition to the IDLE state (`2'b00`)**
  * The `busy` signal must be forced to **0**
  * All internal counters and state must be cleared
* Reset must override:

  * Ongoing multiply/divide operations
  * Clock gating via `diss_clk`
  * Any enable (`EN`) or data (`D`) conditions

No intermediate states, latency, or partial execution is acceptable during reset.
